CLIP zero-shot geometry

D3-04 · section 3 All labs

A toy 2-D version of CLIP's shared embedding space. The image encoder gives one vector, the text encoder one vector per class prompt; the class whose text vector points most nearly the same way as the image wins. Drag the thick image vector or any class vector.

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. The two best cosines are 0.998 and 0.970. Is the prediction 51% or 94% sure? Then set the scale to 10, and to 1.
    why

    Probabilities come from softmax(scale · cos). With CLIP's learned scale of ~100, a cosine gap of 0.03 becomes a logit gap of 3, i.e. odds of e^3 = 20 : 1. Real CLIP cosines are all small (about 0.2-0.35 for matching pairs in 512-D), so without the large scale every prediction would be close to uniform; the scale is learned as a temperature during contrastive training.

  2. Press add class and drag the new vector between the image and the beagle. What did you retrain?
    why

    Nothing. The classifier is the matrix of text embeddings: one row per class, computed from a sentence. A new class is a new row. That is what "zero-shot" means: no image of the new class was used.

  3. Drag the Siamese and Persian prompts together and move the image between them. What does this say about breeds?
    why

    Two text vectors that point almost the same way split the probability, and a small move of the image flips the prediction. Fine-grained classes whose names the text encoder embeds close together (many dog and cat breeds) are exactly where zero-shot CLIP makes most of its mistakes.

  4. Switch the features to raw, no norm. Which class wins, and why? Then choose raw + norm.
    why

    Encoder outputs have different lengths. Without normalisation the dot product |img| |txt| cos θ rewards long text vectors (the pug prompt is the longest) regardless of direction. L2-normalising both sides keeps only the direction, which is why zero_shot_classify normalises first.

  5. Turn on prompt ensembling, press re-word the prompts a few times. What moves more: hollow dot or arrow?
    why

    Each wording drifts randomly around the class's true direction (dashed, known only in this toy). The average of 4 normalised prompts drifts about half as much (1/sqrt(4)), so the class direction is more stable and accuracy goes up: the notebook's toy space gains ~10 points; real CLIP gains 1-5 because real templates make correlated errors.
