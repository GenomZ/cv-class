Learning-rate schedule designer

D1-02 · section 5 All labs

A schedule is a function lr(step). Pick one, shape it, and drag the cursor to read the LR at any step with the formula filled in. The default is the section-7 recipe run: peak 3e-3, 15 % warm-up, 10 epochs of 31 steps.

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Pick one-cycle. Before looking: at what LR does it start, and where does it end? Then tick the log axis.
    answer

    It starts at peak / 25 (div_factor=25), not at 0, rises to the peak at pct_start, and ends at peak / (25 * 10⁴) (final_div_factor=1e4): 4 decades below the start, invisible on a linear axis. Both phases are cosine-shaped, torch's default anneal_strategy="cos"; Smith's original one-cycle used straight lines. Torch also cycles the momentum 0.95 -> 0.85 -> 0.95 (not drawn).

  2. With warm-up + cosine, drag the cursor halfway through the decay phase. What fraction of the peak is left? And at 90 % of the way?
    answer

    Exactly 0.5, because cos(π/2) = 0; at 90 % only (1 + cos(0.9π)) / 2 = 2.4 %. Cosine stays near the peak for a long time, then drops quickly to almost zero: long exploration, short settling.

  3. Your run hits its time cap at 60 % of the planned epochs (6 of 10). Drag the cursor there: what was the LR with warm-up + cosine? With step decay?
    answer

    About 45 % of the peak with cosine, 10 % with step decay: the low-LR phase that settles into a minimum never happened. Schedules that depend on total must be given the number of steps you will really run.

  4. Set the warm-up to 0 and put the cursor on step 0. What learning rate does the network get on its first step, with random weights?
    why

    The full peak. Early gradients are large and noisy, BatchNorm statistics and Adam's moment estimates are not settled yet; warm-up ramps the LR so the first steps cannot throw the weights somewhere bad. The (step + 1) in the warm-up branch makes step 0 use lr_max / warmup instead of a wasted step at 0.
