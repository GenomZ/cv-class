Anchor matching

D2-02 · sections 1-2 All labs

Every feature-map cell carries the same family of anchors. Each anchor gets a training label from its best IoU with any ground-truth box: positive, ignored or negative. Drag or resize the boxes, hover a cell to see its anchors, and change the anchor generator.

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Before reading the tiles: two objects, 2304 anchors. How many anchors are positive, and what is the positive : negative ratio?
    why it matters

    6 positives against 2169 negatives, about 1 : 360. A loss averaged over all anchors would be won by "always background". The RPN therefore samples 256 anchors per image with at most half positives (here: 6 positives + 250 negatives); one-stage detectors use all anchors and fix the imbalance with focal loss (D2-03).

  2. Untick allow low-quality matches. How many positive anchors does the small box (GT 2) keep?
    answer

    None: its best anchor has IoU 0.45, far below 0.7, so the RPN would never be trained to propose this object. The low-quality rule makes the best anchor of every GT positive anyway (the "forced" chip): a weak target, but better than none. Hover its cell: even the smallest 32 px anchor is much larger than the 20 x 24 px object.

  3. With low-quality matches off, switch the stride from 16 to 32 and to 64. Predict what happens to the anchor count and to GT 1's positives.
    why

    The count drops 4x per step (2304, 576, 144): it is (H/s)(W/s)A. Fewer centres means the nearest centre can be up to s/2 away from the object's centre, so the best IoU drops and GT 1 keeps a single positive. Stride 8 does the opposite: 9216 anchors, 18 positives for GT 1.

  4. Rescue the small box without the low-quality rule: which scale chip and which stride give it a real positive (IoU ≥ 0.7)? What does it cost?
    answer

    It needs an anchor of its own size (add 24) and a centre close enough to its centre (stride 8): then it gets a real positive (IoU 0.71). The price: A goes from 9 to 12 and the anchor count from 9216 to 12288, almost all of them extra negatives. This is why FPN (D2-03) puts small anchors only on the fine levels.

  5. Drag the positive threshold down to 0.5 (what the notebook uses for its 128 px images). Then set the negative threshold equal to it. What disappears?
    why

    At 0.5 the positives jump from 6 to 44: 0.7 assumes objects that are large compared with the stride. With both thresholds equal the ignore band vanishes, so anchors that half-cover an object are trained as background or as foreground by a coin flip of 0.01 IoU. Faster R-CNN leaves them out of the loss instead.
