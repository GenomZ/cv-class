Box encoding

D2-02 · section 3 All labs

A positive anchor regresses a target t: the ground truth's offset in units of the anchor's size, and the log of the size ratio. Drag the anchor and the ground truth to change t; move the delta sliders (or the blue dots) to change the prediction d and see where it decodes to.

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Make the ground truth exactly twice as wide as the anchor, then exactly half as wide (drag its right edge, read gw / aw). What are tw in the two cases?
    why

    log 2 = +0.693 and log 1/2 = -0.693: growing and shrinking by the same factor give targets of the same size, so the regression head treats them alike. The linear code (gw - aw) / aw (grey line in the formula box) gives +1.00 and -0.50.

  2. Before clicking x2 scale both boxes: which of tx, ty, tw, th will change?
    answer

    None. Every pixel offset doubles, but so does the anchor size it is divided by. One regression head can therefore serve 32 px and 512 px anchors with targets of the same size; with raw pixel offsets the large anchors would dominate the loss.

  3. Click snap deltas to the target, then move the anchor. Where does the decoded box go?
    why

    After the snap the decoded box sits exactly on the ground truth (difference 0.000 px): decode is the exact inverse of encode. Move the anchor and the decoded box moves with it: deltas mean nothing without the anchor they were computed for.

  4. Drag the dw slider to -2.5. Can the decoded width become zero or negative? Now tick show a linear size code and repeat.
    why

    w = aw * exp(dw) is positive for any dw, so any number the network outputs is a valid box. The linear version w = aw * (1 + dw) turns negative for dw < -1: an impossible box the loss would have to prevent.

  5. Push one prediction far off (a blue dot at 1.5 from its target). With beta = 0.11, how much larger is its L2 gradient than its smooth-L1 gradient? Then raise beta to 1.
    why

    Smooth-L1 is linear beyond beta (gradient 1, whatever the error); L2/2 has gradient equal to the error, 1.5 here and growing. A few badly matched anchors cannot dominate the batch. Below beta the curve is quadratic, so the gradient fades smoothly near the optimum instead of the constant push of L1. A larger beta widens that gentle zone.
