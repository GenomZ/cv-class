Patches and attention

D3-04 · section 1 All labs

A ViT cuts the image into p x p patches and treats them as a sequence of tokens. One attention layer then lets every patch take a weighted average over all patches, with weights computed from their content. Click a patch to make it the query; hover another to see its weight.

the sequence

one patch = one token

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Click a patch of the dark uniform. Will the patches that get its weight be its neighbours, or anywhere?
    why

    Anywhere: the weights depend only on how similar the embeddings are, so dark patches in the background get as much weight as the neighbours. One attention layer already reaches every patch (a global receptive field). A 3 x 3 convolution sees 3 x 3 pixels per layer; stacked, its view grows by 2 pixels per layer, so it needs about 64 layers to span this 128 px image.

  2. Switch p from 16 to 8, then to 32. How many tokens, and how many attention weights per layer?
    why

    N = (128 / p)^2: 256, 64 or 16 tokens (+1 [class]). The attention matrix has (N + 1)^2 entries per head and layer: about 66 000 at p = 8 but only 289 at p = 32. Halving p quadruples N and multiplies the attention cost by 16; that is why ViTs use 14-16 px patches and why Swin restricts attention to windows.

  3. Turn on shuffle the patches (position strength 0). Do the query's weights change? Then try strength 2.
    why

    With no position information they do not change at all (max |Δw| = 0): each patch keeps its weight, it just moves with its patch. Attention is permutation equivariant: shuffle the tokens and the outputs are shuffled the same way. Without position embeddings a ViT sees a bag of patches; adding them (the notebook shows the learned ones) is what lets it know where a patch is.

  4. Drag the temperature to 0.25, then to 4. Watch the top weight and the effective # of patches.
    why

    Dividing the logits by T < 1 sharpens the softmax towards the single most similar patch (the output becomes a copy of it); T > 1 flattens it towards a plain average of all patches. The 1/sqrt(d) in the formula is a fixed temperature that keeps dot products of d-dimensional vectors from saturating the softmax.

  5. Set the position strength to 3 and click a few queries. What changed, and why is it only a soft locality?
    why

    The position term adds a bonus for nearby patches, so attention concentrates around the query but similar patches far away can still win. A trained ViT learns such a bias in its early layers (local heads) and drops it in later ones. Remember these embeddings are hand-made (colour, contrast, edges); a trained ViT learns what to attend to, see the notebook.
