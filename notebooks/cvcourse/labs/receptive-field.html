Receptive field explorer

D1-01 · section 6 All labs

Each unit looks at a small window of the layer below, which looks at a window of the layer below it, and so on. Build a stack, then click any unit (left) or input position (right) to see every input pixel it depends on.

add a layer on top

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Stack conv 3x3, maxpool 2, conv 3x3 (clear, then add). Is the top unit's receptive field 7, 8 or 9 pixels wide?
    answer

    8. The first conv gives 3, the pool adds (2 - 1) * 1 = 1, and after the pool one step in the feature map is 2 input pixels (jump 2), so the last 3x3 adds (3 - 1) * 2 = 4. Layers after a downsampling step grow the receptive field twice as fast: that is why the table's j column matters.

  2. Pick the preset two 3x3, click the top unit, then one 5x5. Same receptive field? How many weights, and how many non-linearities?
    why

    Both see 5 x 5 pixels. Two 3x3 layers use 2 * 9 = 18 weights per channel pair and have a ReLU in between; the 5x5 uses 25 and has one. Three 3x3 = one 7x7 with 27 instead of 49 weights. This is VGG's argument for using only 3x3 kernels.

  3. Look at the shading on the input grid. Are all pixels inside the receptive field equally important to the unit?
    why

    No. The darker the cell, the more paths lead from that pixel to the unit (for all-ones weights it is exactly the gradient of the unit with respect to the pixel). Centre pixels are reached by many paths, border pixels by one, so the effective receptive field is a blob much smaller than the r x r square (Luo et al., 2016).

  4. Pick the preset three dilated. r = 13 with only 27 weights. Which input pixels does the top unit actually read?
    why

    Only every second pixel in each direction (49 of 169): with dilation 2 at every layer, the taps never land on odd offsets. This "gridding" is why dilated stacks start with an ordinary conv or mix rates. Click + conv 3x3 on top of a fresh stack first and add the dilated layers after it: the holes disappear.

  5. Click a unit at the very edge of the top layer. What are the dashed cells?
    why

    Zero padding: part of that unit's receptive field lies outside the image, so it sees fewer real pixels and a lot of zeros. Border units behave differently from centre units, one reason for the bright frame you saw in the learned feature maps in section 8.
