  1. Make P the same size as G and slide it sideways until it covers exactly half of G's width. Predict the IoU before you look.
        
    answer
    1/3, not 1/2. The intersection is half a box, the union one and a half boxes: 0.5 / 1.5.
          IoU reaches 0.5 (the usual "hit" threshold) only when the shift is w/3: intersection 2/3, union 4/3.

  2. Pick disjoint, arrows for IoU, then drag P further away. What happens to 1−IoU, its slope and the arrows? Now switch the arrows to GIoU and DIoU.
        
    why
    Once the boxes stop overlapping, IoU is 0 wherever P is: 1−IoU is a flat line at 1, slope exactly 0, no arrows.
          A network trained with it gets no signal. GIoU still changes because it penalises the empty part of C: its gradient
          makes P grow, most strongly on the edge facing G, until the boxes overlap and IoU takes over (slow, which is why DIoU
          was proposed). DIoU penalises the centre distance ρ²/c², so it slides the whole box straight at G.
    3. 
      
  3. Pick one inside the other and move P around inside G without touching G's edges. Which of IoU, GIoU, DIoU change?
        
    why
    IoU and GIoU do not move: C is G itself and the union is G, so GIoU = IoU and neither knows where P sits.
          Only DIoU (and CIoU) change, through ρ. This blind spot of GIoU is exactly why DIoU was proposed.

  4. Pick same centre, other shape. Which metric is lower than DIoU here, and why? Then make P square.
        
    why
    ρ = 0, so DIoU = IoU. CIoU subtracts αv, where v measures the aspect-ratio mismatch (tall G, wide P).
          With matching aspect ratios v = 0 and CIoU = DIoU again.

  5. The presets are the notebook's corner cases drawn 3× larger, yet every IoU equals the one in the notebook figure. Which numbers
        here would change if everything were scaled by 2 again?
        
    answer
    Areas grow ×4, IoU / GIoU / DIoU / CIoU do not change at all (ratios of areas and of squared lengths):
          the IoU family is scale invariant. The L1 loss doubles: the same relative error costs more on a big box.
