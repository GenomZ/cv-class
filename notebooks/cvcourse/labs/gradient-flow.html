Try this - predict first, then check

  1. PyTorch default init, no BN: slide the depth to 60. How much of the gradient reaches the plain net's first layer?
    why

    Each plain block multiplies the backward signal by about 0.4 (the "gain / block" tile; theory 0.41), so after 58 blocks the first layer gets ~10^-23 of it: it cannot learn. In the residual net the gradient also takes the path through every "+ h": each block multiplies it by (I + dF/dh) instead of dF/dh, about 1.08, never close to 0.

  2. Switch to He init (no BN). Does the plain net still lose its gradient? Does the residual net stay flat?
    why

    He init sets Var(w) = 2/n so that n·Var(w)/2 = 1: the plain net now keeps its gradient on average (that was the point of He et al. 2015). But the residual net explodes, x√2 per block: the identity and a full-size branch add up, so the variance doubles at every block. Real ResNets therefore put BN in the branch and start its last BN at gamma = 0 (torchvision's zero_init_residual) so that every block starts close to the identity.

  3. Back to the PyTorch default, turn BatchNorm on. Is the plain net fixed now?
    why

    No: the gradient now explodes with depth (about x1.25 per layer here, x1.2 in the notebook's conv net), like the notebook's right-hand panel. BN fixes the size of the forward signal, not the backward one (Yang et al. 2019). Residual + BN stays nearly flat at any depth: that combination is what lets 152-layer ResNets train.

  4. He init, plain, no BN, depth 60: press resample weights a few times. Same first-layer gradient each time?
    why

    On average the gain is exactly 1, but a product of 58 random 32 x 32 matrices fluctuates a lot: over ten draws the first-layer gradient varies by about 30x. Deep, narrow plain nets are unpredictable even with the right init. Now tick BatchNorm and resample: the residual + BN net varies by less than 2x, because each of its blocks adds only a small correction to the identity path.
