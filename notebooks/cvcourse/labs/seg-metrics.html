Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Pick small object and click clear: the model predicts background everywhere. Guess the pixel accuracy before you look.
    why

    29 of 1024 pixels are object, so 995 are correct: accuracy 0.972 for a model that found nothing. IoU and Dice of the object are 0, recall is 0, precision is 0/0 (undefined). Even mIoU over the two classes is 0.486, because the background IoU (0.972) props it up. Accuracy is dominated by the largest class; report per-class IoU.

  2. On large object click copy GT, then shift → once. How much IoU does one pixel cost? Now do the same on thin structure.
    why

    The large blob loses only its left and right boundary columns: IoU about 0.9. The 2 px wide vessel loses half of its width and gains the same amount next to it: TP = FP = FN, so IoU = 1/3. IoU measures error relative to the object's size, so thin structures (vessels, roads, cracks, the Pet border class) are brutally sensitive to 1 px offsets.

  3. Paint anything you like. Can you make Dice smaller than IoU? Watch the point on the curve.
    why

    Never: Dice = 2TP / (2TP + FP + FN) and IoU = TP / (TP + FP + FN) give Dice = 2 IoU / (1 + IoU), which is ≥ IoU with equality only at 0 and 1. Same pixels, same ranking per image; Dice just reads higher (IoU 0.5 = Dice 0.67).

  4. On two blobs, copy GT, then erase the small blob completely with the eraser. Predict the IoU of the object class.
    why

    About 0.88: a whole object is missing, yet the score is "good", because IoU over an image counts pixels, and the big blob has most of them. Counting objects needs instance-level metrics (mask AP in D3-03) or per-object IoU.

  5. On small object copy GT, then dilate twice; then copy GT and erode once. Which of precision / recall drops each time, and what does IoU do?
    why

    Dilating only adds pixels: recall stays 1, precision falls (false alarms around the edge). Eroding only removes pixels: precision stays 1, recall falls. IoU (and Dice) punish both, which is why they are the headline metrics. On the thin structure one erosion deletes the whole prediction.
