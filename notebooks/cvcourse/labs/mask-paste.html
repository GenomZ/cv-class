Mask R-CNN predicts one M x M soft mask per box (M = 28) and stretches it over the box. Here the mask is the best possible one (the fraction of each cell the object covers), so every error comes from the fixed resolution. Drag the box or its corners; hover a pixel.

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Press 340 px, then 32 px: which box gets the higher mask IoU? Then look at the fingers.
    why

    Mask IoU hardly moves (about 0.95 at every size): the shape scales with the box, so the 28 x 28 grid loses the same fraction of its area. But at 340 px one cell is 12 px tall: fingers and gaps thinner than a cell disappear and the edge is off by several pixels, while at 32 px a cell is about one pixel and the mask is as sharp as the image. Area IoU hides this because it is dominated by the interior; the 2-px boundary IoU falls from ~0.96 at 32 px to ~0.6 at 340 px. Coarse, blobby edges on large objects are why PointRend (re-predicts uncertain boundary points at high resolution) and query-based models such as Mask2Former (masks at 1/4 of the image resolution) were built.

  2. With the large box, switch M to 7, then to 56. What survives? Why not always use 56?
    why

    Anything thinner than about one cell is averaged below 0.5 and vanishes: at M = 7 the arms merge with the body, at 56 even the fingers appear. But the mask head runs for every detection (up to 100 per image) and outputs one map per class: 56 x 56 is 4x the outputs, the compute of the up-sampling layers and the memory of the training targets. 28 x 28 was the paper's compromise.

  3. Hover the edge of an arm, then set the threshold to 0.3 and to 0.7. Which way does the edge move?
    why

    The 4 weights are the bilinear weights (they always sum to 1); the pixel is in the mask if the weighted value is at least the threshold. A lower threshold grows the mask, a higher one shrinks it, by a fraction of a cell: a fraction of a pixel in the small box, several pixels in the large one. For this "perfect" mask 0.5 is about optimal.

  4. Switch to threshold → resize with the large box. Smoother or blockier? Compare the mask IoU.
    why

    Blockier, and the IoU drops (about 0.95 to 0.90 for the person): thresholding first keeps only one bit per cell, so the edge can only run along cell borders (a staircase of 12-px steps). The soft values carry where inside a cell the edge is; resizing them first and thresholding afterwards (as paste_mask and torchvision do) keeps that information.

  5. Set head blur to 1.5. Which threshold now gives the best IoU? Where did the fingers go?
    why

    Blurring averages thin parts with the background, so they fall below 0.5 and vanish; lowering the threshold brings them back but bloats the body. A real mask head is never this perfect, which is one more reason the paste-back threshold is a convention, not an optimum.
