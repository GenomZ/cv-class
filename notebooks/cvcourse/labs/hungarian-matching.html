Hungarian matching

D2-04 · section 2 All labs

DETR's N object queries each predict a box and a class. Training first matches them one-to-one to the ground-truth objects with the lowest total cost; matched queries learn their object, all others learn "no object". Drag the blue query boxes (corner = resize, the green ground truth moves too), click one to edit its class.

selected query

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. q4 and q5 both sit on the triangle. After matching, how many of them are trained to predict the triangle? Drag q5 exactly onto q4 and check again.
    why

    One. Matching is one-to-one, so the cheaper of the two takes the triangle and the other is trained towards "no object". A duplicate is punished by the loss, so the trained network learns not to produce duplicates, and DETR needs no NMS. Compare D2-02 / D2-03: there every anchor overlapping an object is a positive, so duplicates are trained in, and NMS has to remove them afterwards.

  2. Turn on compare with greedy: each object, in the order A, B, C, takes its cheapest free query. Before looking: is greedy ever worse than the optimum?
    why

    Yes. A grabs q1, its cheapest, although q1 is the only good candidate for B; B then has to take the poor q3. The optimum gives q1 to B and the slightly worse q2 to A. The Hungarian algorithm (here: an exhaustive search, identical to scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment for this size) minimises the sum.

  3. Raise λcls slowly from 1 to 4. Who gets matched to B, and what happens to the well-placed q1?
    why

    Around λcls = 4 the class term outweighs geometry: q3 says "square" with 0.70 and takes B although its box is far off, and q1 (which says "circle") is left with "no object". The weights decide what "the same object" means. DETR's 1 / 5 / 2 let geometry dominate, which matters early in training when the class probabilities are still nearly uniform.

  4. Show the GIoU term and drag q3, which overlaps nothing, further away. Does its GIoU cost change? Would plain IoU?
    why

    IoU is 0 for every pair of disjoint boxes, so it cannot tell a near miss from a box on the other side of the image. GIoU = IoU - |E \ U| / |E| keeps falling towards -1 as the boxes move apart (E = smallest enclosing box, U = union), so the cost still ranks far queries. That is why DETR uses GIoU (D2-01) next to the L1 term.

  5. Switch the matrix to show only the class term, then only L1. Which term decides the default matching?
    why

    With 1 / 5 / 2 the L1 and GIoU columns span several units while the class term moves by at most 1: geometry decides. The class term breaks ties between boxes that are about equally well placed.
