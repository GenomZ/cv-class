Focal loss

D2-03 · section 5 All labs

Left: one example. Drag the marker along pt (the predicted probability of the true label) and see how much the modulating factor (1 - pt)γ shrinks its loss. Right: what actually matters, the sum over a whole image of anchors, split into the easy negatives and the few hard positives.

Try this - predict first, then check

  1. With the defaults (100 000 easy negatives at pt = 0.99, 20 positives at 0.3): under plain CE, what share of the total loss do the easy negatives make, although each contributes only 0.01? Then compare the focal bar.
    why

    100 000 x 0.01 = 1 005 against 20 x 1.2 = 24: about 98% of the loss, and of the gradient, says "these negatives are negatives", which the network already knows. With γ = 2 each easy negative is multiplied by (1 - 0.99)2 = 0.0001, and the 20 positives now carry about 97% of the loss.

  2. Move the marker to pt = 0.9 and read the factor. Is the "100x less" of the notebook the whole story?
    why

    (1 - 0.9)2 = 0.01 is the 100x. αt adds another factor: 0.25 for positives, 0.75 for negatives. Switch the example type to see both. With γ = 0 the focal loss is just α-weighted CE.

  3. Set the easy negatives' pt to 0.9 instead of 0.99. Does γ = 2 still rescue the positives? What would?
    why

    No: 100 000 x 0.75 x 0.01 x 0.105 = 79 against 3 for the positives, still about 96% negatives. The modulating factor only silences examples that are already very easy. Raising γ helps, and so does fewer negatives, which is why RetinaNet still matches anchors carefully (ignore zone between IoU 0.4 and 0.5).

  4. Set the easy negatives to pt = 0.5: this is a fresh network whose classification bias is 0. Now 0.99.
    why

    At pt = 0.5 every negative is a "hard" example; 100 000 of them swamp the positives under any γ, and the first updates push everything towards background. RetinaNet's bias init b = -log((1 - π)/π), π = 0.01 (section 4) starts every anchor at p = 0.01, so negatives start at pt = 0.99, exactly where γ = 2 works.

  5. Switch the bars to gradient. Is the picture different from the loss?
    why

    The shares move a little but tell the same story. The gradient with respect to the logit is |dFL/dx| = αt(1 - pt)γ ((1 - pt) - γ pt log pt); for CE it is simply 1 - pt. The gradient is what training actually follows.
