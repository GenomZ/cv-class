Try this - predict first, then check

  1. Before looking at the bottom bar: which stage holds most of ResNet-50's parameters, and what share?
    why

    layer4: 15.0 M, 63 %; stem + layer1 together hold under 1 %. Channels double at every stage and a conv's weights grow with C_in·C_out, so the last stage dominates - and the generic early stages you usually freeze are almost free in parameters.

  2. Set until = layer2 (the notebook's regime C). What fraction still trains? How much compute does it save?
    why

    94 % of the parameters still train - freezing the early stages saves little memory for weights. It saves more compute: stem..layer2 run at high resolution and are 44 % of the forward MACs, and the backward pass now stops at layer3 (about 12.3 -> 8.6 GMACs per image).

  3. Full fine-tune, base_lr 3e-4, decay 0.3: what learning rate does the stem get? Is it still learning?
    why

    3e-4 · 0.3^5 = 7.3e-7, 400x less than the head: in practice almost frozen. The decay is a soft version of freezing; decay = 1 gives every group the same LR, decay = 0.1 makes everything below layer3 practically frozen.

  4. resnet18, until = layer2: compare with the notebook's printout "trainable: 10,512,421 of 11,195,493".
    why

    Same numbers: the notebook runs ResNet-18 on a CPU. Its layer4 is 75 % of the model (8.4 M of 11.2 M).

  5. A frozen stage has requires_grad = False. Does its output stay exactly the same during training?
    answer

    Not necessarily: in model.train() mode its BatchNorm layers still update their running mean / variance from every batch, so the features drift even though no weight changes. That is why the notebook calls day1.keep_bn_eval(model).
